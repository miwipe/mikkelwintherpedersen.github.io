About & curriculum vitae

A geographer’s perspective.
A geneticist’s tools.

I study the history of nature through DNA left behind in the environment.

Research & background

I am an Associate Professor at the Globe Institute, University of Copenhagen, and lead the Palaeo-Environmental Genomics Group. My work brings together geography, genetics and bioinformatics to reconstruct past ecosystems and explore their responses to climate and human activity.

My research has contributed to the discovery of a two-million-year-old ecosystem in Greenland and to studies of Arctic biodiversity, early pastoralism and the environments encountered by early human populations.

Fieldwork is an important part of this research. I have led expeditions in Alaska, the United States, Morocco, Canada, northern Sweden and the Republic of the Congo, alongside work with colleagues across the Arctic and beyond.

Teaching & collaboration

I teach the PhD course Ancient Environmental Genomics and supervise students and researchers working across field, laboratory and computational approaches. I am a co-PI in the Ancient Environmental Genomics Initiative for Sustainability (AEGIS) and the Centre for Ancient Environmental Genomics.
